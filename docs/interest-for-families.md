# How Interest Works

Money you keep in your allowance account earns interest, just like a savings account at a bank. The longer you leave money in, the more it earns.

## The rate

The rate is **10% a year**. That means **$100 left alone for a whole year grows to about $110.**

Banks call this kind of rate an **APY** (annual percentage yield). It's the number a bank advertises for a savings account, so you can compare it to a real one.

## When interest is paid

Interest is paid **once a month**. It shows up as a deposit dated **11:59 PM on the last day of the month**, with a green **Interest** badge next to it on your transactions page.

## How much you earn

Interest is based on your **average daily balance** for the month: your balance at the end of each day, averaged over every day in the month.

This means **when** you add or spend money matters, not just how much you have on the last day:

- Money added early in the month earns interest for more days than money added near the end.
- Money spent early in the month stops earning sooner.

Each month earns about one-twelfth of the yearly rate. Longer months earn a little more than shorter ones: at 10% a year, a 31-day month pays about 0.81% of your average balance, a 30-day month about 0.79%, and February about 0.73%.

### Example

Say it's January (31 days):

| Days         | Balance at the end of each day |
| ------------ | ------------------------------ |
| Jan 1–10     | $100.00                        |
| Jan 11–31    | $160.00 (you deposited $60 on Jan 11) |

Your average daily balance is the balance for each day, added up and divided by 31:

- ($100 × 10 days + $160 × 21 days) ÷ 31 days = **$140.65**
- January interest: about 0.81% of $140.65 = **$1.14**

So $1.14 is deposited on January 31. The note on the deposit shows the details: `Interest: 10% APY on avg daily balance $140.65 (Jan 2026)`.

## Interest earns interest too

Each interest deposit becomes part of your balance, so next month you earn interest on it as well. This is called **compounding**. In the example, February starts at $161.14 instead of $160.00.

## Good to know

- **Interest is rounded to the nearest cent.** If a month's interest would be less than half a cent, nothing is posted that month.
- **No interest is charged on a negative balance.** If your average balance for the month is zero or below, you just don't earn interest that month.
- **Days follow Eastern time.** A deposit at 11:30 PM Eastern on the last day of the month counts for that month.
- **The rate can change.** If it does, the new rate only applies from the date it changes. Interest already paid stays the same.
- **Backdated changes aren't recalculated automatically.** If a transaction is added or changed with a date in a month that has already been paid interest, that month's interest doesn't update by itself. Ask a parent to have it recalculated.
